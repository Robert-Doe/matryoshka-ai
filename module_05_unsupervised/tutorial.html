Unsupervised Learning (Finding Structure Without Labels)

No answer key. The model finds groups and axes; you assign the meaning. This is how defenders catch the attacks nobody labeled yet.

⏱ ~75 min🔵 K-Means + PCA 🛡 UEBA / anomaly detection

1 · Two tools

ToolQuestion it answersOutput
Clustering (K-Means)What natural groups exist?a group label per point
Dim. reduction (PCA)Which few axes carry the signal?fewer features, plottable

2 · Clustering: watch K-Means converge

py module_05_unsupervised\kmeans_demo.py

✅ What to watch

  • Inertia (cluster tightness) drops with each iteration then flattens — that flattening is convergence.
  • The elbow method finds k=3 (the true number) where adding clusters stops helping.
  • Cluster labels are arbitrary — 0 isn't "better" than 1. Structure, not meaning.

3 · Dimensionality reduction: 30-D → 2-D

py module_05_unsupervised\pca_demo.py   # writes plots/pca_2d.png

PCA squashes 30 tumor features into 2 numbers that still separate the classes. The explained variance tells you how much information you kept (here ~63% in 2 components; 95% needs 10). Open the PNG — the classes visibly split.

4 · Segmentation + the security payoff

py module_05_unsupervised\customer_segments.py

🛡 The tiny cluster is the alert

The script plants a small group of users with huge off-hours downloads. K-Means isolates them into their own tiny cluster. In UEBA (User & Entity Behavior Analytics), a small cluster far from everything else is a textbook unsupervised anomaly — found with no labeled attack data. This is why clustering matters for defenders: it surfaces the unknown-unknowns.

5 · The mindset

Unsupervised learning is a hypothesis generator. It proposes structure; your domain expertise turns it into a decision. Always keep a human in the loop to name and validate what the machine found.
