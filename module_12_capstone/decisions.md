# Module 12 — DECISIONS

## Decision 1 — A capstone, not a final exam
The course ends with a build, not a test.
- **Why:** Recall proves little; end-to-end application proves mastery. Every prior
  module taught one piece in isolation — the capstone forces the student to join them,
  which is where real understanding (and real difficulty) lives. It's also a portfolio
  artifact and a rehearsal for applied research work.

## Decision 2 — Three tracks, one standard of rigor
ML / Vision / LLM tracks, each judged by the same rubric.
- **Why:** Students have different interests and prior modules resonate differently.
  Letting them choose the *subject* keeps motivation high, while holding the
  *rigor* (baseline, honest evaluation, ethics, write-up) constant ensures the
  learning outcome is the same regardless of track.

## Decision 3 — Weight evaluation, ethics, and communication over modeling
The rubric gives 55/100 to eval + ethics + communication, 15 to modeling.
- **Why:** This is the course's whole thesis made into a grade. The scarce, valuable,
  transferable skills are measuring without self-deception, reasoning about harm, and
  explaining clearly. Beginners over-index on model performance; the rubric deliberately
  corrects that, and makes the correction explicit so the student internalizes it.

## Decision 4 — Ship a runnable skeleton, a brief, a rubric, and a write-up template
Four scaffolding artifacts, not just an assignment prompt.
- **Why:** "Build an end-to-end project" is paralyzing from a blank page. The skeleton
  (`main.py`) encodes the six-stage pipeline as TODOs and runs immediately; the rubric
  sets the target; the write-up template removes the blank-page problem for the report.
  Reducing friction on structure lets the student spend effort on the actual work.

## Decision 5 — Fill the write-up AS you go
The template and brief both push writing during, not after, the build.
- **Why:** Writing-as-you-go keeps the student honest about what they actually did and
  found, prevents retroactive rationalization, and mirrors good research practice (a lab
  notebook, not a post-hoc story). The write-up is where a pile of code becomes a project.

## Decision 6 — Loop the capstone back through the ethics red-team
"Done" requires running the Module 11 checklist on your own model.
- **Why:** It makes responsibility a build step, not an afterthought, and reinforces the
  course's throughline that capability and responsibility are inseparable. For this
  student, it also cements the security-as-AI-audit framing one last time.

## What we deliberately did NOT do
- **No fixed dataset or single "correct" project** — open-endedness is the point; real
  work starts from an ill-defined problem, and choosing/scoping it is part of the skill.
- **No leaderboard / accuracy target** — optimizing a single number is exactly the
  anti-pattern the rubric is built to discourage.
- **No grading automation** — judgment (is the evaluation honest? is the harm real?)
  can't be auto-graded, and learning to self-assess against the rubric is itself a
  target outcome.
